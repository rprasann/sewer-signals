**Subject: Weekly COVID-19 Surveillance Update – Bay Area Forecast**

The nine-county San Francisco Bay Area is entering a period of rising COVID-19 case trajectories. Over the next four weeks, all forecasted counties show an upward trend in new cases. This increase is most pronounced in Alameda and San Francisco counties, where the forecast shows a rapid surge in case numbers. While other counties like Santa Clara and Contra Costa are also seeing growth, the rate of increase in Alameda is particularly sharp, signaling a significant regional shift.

When reviewing these forecasts, you will notice that some counties have much wider "prediction intervals" than others. In plain language, this width represents our level of uncertainty. A wide interval—such as the one seen in Alameda—means that while we expect cases to rise, the exact number could vary significantly. For decision-makers, a wide interval serves as a reminder to prepare for a broad range of possibilities. Rather than planning for a single specific number, we should use these wide ranges to ensure we have enough surge capacity to handle the higher end of the potential case volume.

A critical component of this forecast is the "lead-time" provided by wastewater monitoring. Because the model uses viral signals found in sewage to predict clinical cases, it is essentially providing an early warning system. This means the surge is being detected in the community before people begin showing up at clinics or testing centers. This lead-time is highly actionable; it provides public health officials with a window of opportunity to prepare hospital staffing, resource allocation, and community messaging before the clinical surge reaches its peak.

While specific data on which individual factors (such as seasonality or social movement) are driving these changes is not detailed in this brief, the model's current accuracy is tied to the biological signals detected in wastewater. This implies that the current trend is being driven by viral activity already circulating within the community. We will continue to monitor these signals to refine our preparedness strategies as the surge progresses.
