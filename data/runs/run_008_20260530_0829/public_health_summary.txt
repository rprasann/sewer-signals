For decision-makers, this means preparing for a range of scenarios: while we should plan for the upward trend, we must also maintain enough flexibility to manage the higher-end possibilities presented by these wide intervals.

The ability to forecast these trends relies on "lead-time" signals from wastewater monitoring. Because virus levels in sewage typically rise before people begin showing symptoms or seeking clinical testing, these signals act as an early warning system. This lead time is highly actionable; it allows public health officials to prepare for a surge in clinical cases before they actually arrive at clinics and hospitals. By recognizing these signals now, we can proactively manage resources such as testing supplies and healthcare staffing in anticipation of the coming weeks.
